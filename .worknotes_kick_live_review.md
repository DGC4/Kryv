# Kick Live review working notes

**Scope:** Non-intrusive observation of Kick’s public desktop home and a public live-channel surface in the connected browser. No messages, follows, subscriptions, purchases, account-profile prompts, reward claims, or commercial actions were submitted.

## Home and discovery observations

- The home route presents a persistent left navigation (Home, Browse, Following, Drops) plus a collapsible followed/recommended-channel rail, independent from the main discovery canvas.
- The top discovery module is a featured live carousel. The featured stream includes concurrent-viewer count, creator identity, stream title, category, language, a follow affordance, a dedicated **Watch now** action, muting, and a chat-preview toggle.
- The initial feed uses a skeleton layout before populated content arrives. Cards reserve space, which reduces layout shift while imagery and metadata load.
- The primary discovery rail is explicitly **Streams For You**. Cards identify a live state, viewer count, creator, title, category, language, and sometimes a visible `18+` label.
- Category discovery exposes total watchers and a lightweight genre/tag hierarchy. A category grid is followed by category-specific horizontal rails and a Popular Clips rail.
- Footer information segregates company, help, creator/developer, legal, privacy/cookie, drops, and advertising-policy/contact paths. This makes operator, creator, and advertiser entry points discoverable without overloading the live surface.

## Live channel observations

- The public channel route has a large primary video canvas, a fixed right chat panel on desktop, a persistent left channel-navigation rail, and stream identity/actions below the player.
- The channel identity area places current live state, channel/avatar identity, title, category, language, verified-status indicator when applicable, live viewer count, and a sharing affordance near the player.
- Commercial actions are visually separated as **Follow**, **Gift Subs**, and **Subscribe**. They are distinct from chat and stream controls, which is a useful non-deceptive pattern.
- The chat panel has a visible connected/user-status state, emotes, a moderated/system message treatment, a new-message divider, viewer/user chips, a settings affordance, and an entry boundary (`Followers only` was visible in this observation). The controls make it possible to see that participation eligibility is channel-governed before a message is entered.
- The observed presentation shows a loading indicator inside the player before playback begins; it keeps the video surface at a stable size while chat loads separately.

## Transferable requirements for original Kryv Live

1. Preserve a stable three-region desktop layout: collapsible LiveGuide, responsive KryvPlayer stage, and independently loadable LiveChat pane. On narrow layouts, the chat pane should become a bottom sheet or tab rather than shrinking the player below a usable size.
2. Use card-level facts that help a viewer decide quickly: `LIVE`, current viewers, creator, descriptive title, category, language, maturity, and content-access state. Do not infer age suitability solely in the client.
3. Make follow, subscription, gift, tip, advertising disclosure, sharing, and chat entry visibly separate. Each requires independently authorized API operations and must never be triggered by a player gesture.
4. Build chat eligibility as a server-authoritative `ChatAccessState`, representing follower-delay, subscriber-only, account-age, timeout, ban, slow mode, verified-account, and moderator state. The composer must render the applicable rule before accepting input.
5. Keep the player and chat independently fault-tolerant. A chat websocket/realtime failure cannot block stream playback; a stream unavailable/reconnect state cannot erase moderation and reporting controls.
6. Build an owned stream-loading skeleton and use aspect-ratio reservation for player, channel identity, guide cards, and chat, with abortable image/data requests.
7. Any future free-tier advertising placement must be explicit, safely separated from organic stream cards, and policy-decided on the server. The user’s requested free-tier surface policy covers Cinema, Watch, and Live; this observation does not justify activating delivery.

## Public advertising-policy observations

Kick’s published advertising guidelines treat paid video, display, and branded integrations as advertising and assign responsibility for legal and community-policy compliance to the advertiser. The page requires a clearly identified advertiser or payer, prohibits false or deceptive advertising, and explicitly rejects manipulative user-interface practices such as concealed close controls, false urgency, and materially asymmetric accept/reject choices.

The policy also states privacy-by-design expectations: express consent is required before collection of personal data, fingerprinting and unauthorized cross-platform tracking are disallowed, and collection of special-category data is prohibited. It includes restrictions on unlicensed or age-inappropriate gambling, minors, unsafe software, counterfeit goods, deceptive schemes, weapon/illegal-product promotion, and certain health claims. It additionally requires clear disclosure for realistic synthetic or AI-generated media that depicts real people or events deceptively, while prohibiting unauthorized digital-twin/voice-clone endorsements.

### Kryv guardrails derived from the public policy review

| Guardrail | Required Kryv implementation behavior |
|---|---|
| Advertiser disclosure | Every paid creative has a persisted advertiser/payer identity and a high-contrast, localized disclosure adjacent to its placement; paid content must never use the organic content-card component alone. |
| User autonomy | Skip, close, consent-refusal, and report controls are focusable, touch-operable, visually available when required by placement policy, and cannot be concealed by creative markup. No countdown may claim urgency unrelated to actual media duration. |
| Privacy | The ad decision service receives a minimized, pseudonymous eligibility context. It must not accept browser storage identifiers, cross-site fingerprint data, or special-category targeting attributes. |
| Consent | The server resolves consent before selecting targeted/measurement behavior. A missing or withdrawn consent state defaults to conservative, contextual, or no-ad behavior according to policy; clients cannot override it. |
| Creative safety | A creative must be owner-reviewed, rights-attested, scanned, region/maturity-classified, expiration-bounded, and revocable before eligibility. Assets must never contain executable third-party tracking code. |
| Sensitive content | Age-, location-, and content-suitability restrictions are evaluated server-side for each Cinema, Watch, and Live surface. Restricted categories remain ineligible until dedicated policy, controls, and approval evidence exist. |
| Synthetic media | Store an explicit synthetic-media declaration and rights-attestation state. Require persistent visible labeling when a creative includes realistic synthetic depictions; block unlicensed likeness or voice claims. |
| Measurement | Use signed, short-lived, idempotent event authorizations for permitted exposure and engagement events. Treat click URLs as allowlisted, reviewed redirects; do not emit raw account, profile, or session identifiers. |
| Operations | Retain the global hard-disabled runtime switch, campaign and creative pause controls, audit trail, and a tested fail-closed behavior. A serving failure must return no creative rather than fall back to unreviewed content. |

**Source:** `https://kick.com/advertising-policy`, observed 2026-08-20 in the connected browser.
