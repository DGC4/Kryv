# YouTube Watch product review — working notes

## Home, discovery, and loading state

The YouTube home surface starts with skeleton cards that reserve thumbnail, avatar, and text geometry while feed data loads. Once hydrated, it keeps a compact global masthead with a guide control, logo/home return, broad search field, voice-search affordance, creator entry, notifications, and account menu. A persistent left guide provides top-level modes, subscriptions, and policy/support links.

Discovery is split into a horizontally scrollable topical-chip row and a vertically extensible mixed feed. Each content card clearly distinguishes thumbnail, duration, creator/channel, title, view count, relative time, and an overflow menu. Sponsored items are explicitly labelled and visually separated. Kryv Watch should adopt the transferable principles—ratio-stable skeletons, explicit content type/advertising labels, creator identity, duration/progress metadata, readable overflow actions, and a stable route shell—using original branding and components.

## Initial Kryv Watch implications

1. Implement responsive skeleton cards before content fetches to prevent layout shifts.
2. Keep Watch discovery distinct from Cinema rails: creator/video cards need channel identity, view/activity context, duration, and publication/live state.
3. Use a compact top search plus filters/chips, but support keyboard navigation and horizontal touch scrolling without trapping focus.
4. Label paid/promoted content and age-sensitive content explicitly; do not blur editorial and paid placement.

## Search, filters, and ad treatment

The result page distinguishes sponsored placements at the card level and maintains separate chips for fast refinements such as type, watched state, recency, and live status. Its expanded filters group content type, duration, recency, capability/features, and sort in a lightweight popover. Results carry duration, creator, view/activity context, chapters/quality labels where applicable, and clearly labelled paid or fundraiser states.

On the sampled watch page, an in-player sponsored placement shows an explicit `Sponsored` label, advertiser identity/domain, a distinct commercial call-to-action, ad-control entry point, and a dedicated skip control. Sidebar sponsored cards are also visibly labelled and separated from organic recommendations. Kryv should transfer the safeguards, not the surface: every paid unit must have a standardized disclosure, ad decision identifier, duration/skip policy, user ad-controls entry, context-safe placement, frequency cap, profile/maturity exclusion, and a non-deceptive boundary between organic/recommended and paid content. Kryv should never auto-open advertiser links, and all ad CTA links must use safe external-navigation protections.

## Watch-page architecture implications

The player has distinct modes and controls (seek, captions, settings, theater, full screen), a content context below the player, and a contextual recommendation rail beside it. Kryv Watch needs a reusable player shell that separates playback state from metadata, recommendations, comments/chat, sponsorship state, and creator actions. It must preserve keyboard shortcuts, focus visibility, mobile controls, and an original privacy/advertising controls model.

## Player controls, creator context, and discussion

The sampled watch player exposes captions, sleep timer, playback speed, and quality selection through a compact settings menu, alongside theatre/full-screen controls and keyboard-labelled player actions. Beneath the player, the creator identity, subscription control, engagement actions, save/share/overflow controls, description expansion, and a separately loaded comment surface are all distinct from media playback. Related-content chips frame the recommendation sidebar and preserve context from search, series, channel, and topic.

Kryv Watch should model these as separately authorized modules: playback preference state; creator-follow/subscription state; viewer library actions; report/moderation action; context tags; and comments/chat with independent moderation. It should expose quality selection only when the provider supports it, respect data saver/reduced motion preferences, retain a safe `Auto` option, and avoid claiming availability or captions that do not exist.
